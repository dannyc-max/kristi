# Calls from Inside the House: mailing list, pricing and content plan

Fall 2026 into spring 2027. The Apple Harvest Festival (Sat Oct 17, downtown Waynesville) is the first big day for collecting emails.

## 1. Sign-up incentive: the free bookmark pattern

**Ridgeline Bookmarks** (`print/Ridgeline-Bookmarks-free-pattern.pdf`): a one-page mini version of the landscape pattern. You layer fabrics with Elmer's glue or a pattern glue stick, let it dry, stitch by machine or by hand, then cut it into three 2″ × 6″ bookmarks and zigzag the edges. It's digital, so it costs nothing to give away. The bottom of the page points people to the full 10″ × 10″ pattern.

At the booth:

- **Sign:** "Free bookmark pattern when you join the list."
- **Show a finished set** of three bookmarks next to the clipboard so people can see what they'll make.
- **Sell finished bookmarks** as an impulse buy ($8–12 each, or 3 for $25). The costing sheet already has bookmarks at $6–12. People who buy one are good candidates for the free pattern, and people who get the pattern may come back for a kit.
- **Collect addresses** on the clipboard sheet (`print/signup-sheet.html`) and type them into your email tool that night.

**Email tool:** MailerLite or Kit. Both have free plans that cover a list this size, and both can send the welcome email automatically. Paste the tool's form URL into `SIGNUP_URL` in `index.html` and the website form will work.

## 2. Rhythm: every 4–6 weeks, when there's a reason

The automatic welcome email (`docs/welcome-email.md`) goes out the moment someone signs up. After that, send an email only when there's a real reason. That works out to about every 4–6 weeks. Here are timely reasons, starting two weeks after the festival:

| Send around | Reason | Topic |
|---|---|---|
| **Tue Nov 3** | Thanksgiving is Nov 26, and orders need time to ship | **Set the table.** Runners and placemats styled for a holiday table. Subscribers see new colorways a day before Etsy. Thank-you and photos from the festival. |
| **Tue Dec 1** | Giving Tuesday, just after Small Business Saturday (Nov 28) | **Handmade gifts.** Bookmarks as stocking stuffers (link to the free pattern again), kits for the sewist in their life, throws. Etsy holiday shipping cutoff (check Etsy's posted date). |
| **Mid-January** (around Jan 12) | New year, buy-less resolutions, cold weather | **Make do and mend.** The 2012 upcycling posts brought back: an old sweater turned into leg warmers, a man's shirt into a toddler dress. Ties to the upcycled kids' dresses you sell now. |
| **February**, around QuiltCon | QuiltCon 2027 (check the dates) | **Soft materials, loud opinions.** Protest and political quilts you admire (credit the makers), what you're bringing or hoping to see, a new Stitch Library card. |
| **Around Sat Mar 20** | National Quilting Day is the third Saturday in March | **Spring dye day.** New colorways, a process reel, and readers' bookmark photos if anyone sends them. |
| **Around Apr 22** | Earth Day | **Scraps and salvage.** Upcycled denim, how you use dye-batch leftovers, spring fair dates. |

Each email has the same three parts, so it's quick to write:

1. **One story:** a process moment, a piece, or a quilt you admired (credit the maker).
2. **One thing to buy:** usually runners or placemats, linked to Etsy.
3. **One place to find you:** the next fair, or "the shop is open 24/7."

Skip a month if there's nothing to say. Sending less often keeps people opening the emails.

## 4. What other artists sell, and at what prices

*Note: this session's network couldn't open artists' websites directly, so this comes from search results that cache their pages. Some prices may be out of date.*

**Judy Simmons** (Fletcher, NC; Southern Highland Craft Guild member) sells on [her own website](http://www.judysimmonsfiberart.com/) and through the Guild shops.

- **Small Works**, about 12″ × 15″ to 15″ × 18″: **$135–175** ("Old Tree," 15 × 18, $175; "The Last Leaf," $135).
- **Botanicals**, around 22″ × 28″: **$450–1,250** ("Sweet Nectar," "Morning's Glory").
- **How her site is set up:** separate gallery pages for each series (Small Works, Botanicals). Every piece lists its size, materials and price, or "Sold." There's also a workshops page.

**What to borrow from her site:**

- **Show size, materials and price on every piece.** Mark sold pieces "Sold" rather than taking them down. It shows people buy your work.
- **Group work into series.** For you: the Tarot series, Blue Ridge landscapes, and Small works.
- **Add a teaching page later.** You teach already (the Local Cloth sewing camp), and your kits and patterns fit there.

**Bernie Rowell** (Asheville area, Guild member for more than 30 years) sells original painted quilts and **limited edition prints** through the Folk Art Center and her studio. Prints or cards of your quilts would make a lower-priced way into your art. I couldn't find her current prices.

**Benchmarks**

- **Per square inch:** about **$1 per square inch** is a common art quilt benchmark for gallery sales, where galleries take a 30–50% commission. Artists often discount about 25% when selling directly. Graybeard at $770 for 33″ × 35″ is about $0.67 per square inch, right in the direct-sale range.
- **Etsy runners and placemats:** quilted placemat sets of 4 list around **$27–68**, and quilted runners around **$25–62**. Most are commercial fabric. Hand-dyed can sit at or above the top of those ranges.

## 5. Stitch Library: a page for each technique

The website now has a **Stitch Library** page (`techniques.html`), linked from the homepage. Each technique from the pattern gets its own card:

- **Raw edge** and **Turned edge**
- **Decorative quilting** and **Couching**
- **Wool roving under tulle**, **Sequin mesh** and **French knots**
- **Hand stitches**
- **Finishing** (zigzag edge, binding, hanger, rod pocket, shadow box)

Every card follows the same layout:

- A photo or reel
- Skill level
- What the technique is and what you need
- 3–4 short steps and one tip
- Which step of the full pattern covers it

Why this works:

- **Each card is newsletter content.** "New in the Stitch Library: couching" is a reason to send an email.
- **It sells the pattern without being pushy.** Every card points back to it.
- **People search for these techniques by name** ("how to couch yarn on a quilt"), so the pages can bring in new visitors over time.
- **Each card pairs with a Reel.** Film the technique once and use it in all three places.

To grow it later: when a card gets long, give it its own page with more photos.

## 6. YouTube and Instagram

**YouTube (Kristi Sews Cool Stuff, 2021 sew-alongs):**

- The site shows these as "Tapes from the basement," old footage from your first channel.
- Cut 30–60 second clips of the best moments (French seams, the pattern unfolding "like a marshmallow in a microwave") and post them as Reels.
- If you start posting quilt videos, rename the channel to match the brand.

**Instagram:** use the same names everywhere:

- **Dye Day:** cloth going in white and coming out blue.
- **Ridge by Ridge:** a landscape built layer by layer, sped up.
- **Stitch Library:** one technique per reel, matching the website cards.
- **Soft materials, loud opinions:** protest and political quilts you admire, credited and tagged.
