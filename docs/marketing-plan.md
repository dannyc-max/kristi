# Calls from Inside the House: mailing list and content plan

Fall 2026. Built around the Apple Harvest Festival (Sat Oct 17, downtown Waynesville) as the first big list-building day.

## 1. The giveaway: the "Evidence Bag"

Everyone who signs up at the booth gets a small zip bag of hand-dyed fabric scraps with a printed label:

> **EVIDENCE** · Recovered from inside the house · Case #___ · Do not open (unless you sew)

Why it works:

- **Almost free.** The scraps are dye-batch offcuts you'd otherwise toss. Small zip bags run a few cents each, and the labels print on plain sticker paper.
- **On brand.** It's the horror-movie joke in a bag, and it shows off the hand-dyed color.
- **Quilters love scraps.** It's a real thing they'll use, not swag.
- **It starts conversations.** People ask what's in the bag, and that's your pitch.

Stack two more no-cost rewards on top of it:

1. **A free mini pattern, sent in the welcome email.** Trim the Landscape Art Quilt booklet down to a 5″ mug-rug version ("Mini Ridgeline"). Its only cost is your time to make the PDF once. It also teaches people to open your emails.
2. **A placemat drawing at the end of the day.** Draw one hand-dyed placemat (materials cost only) from everyone who signed up and announce the winner in the first email. Even people who signed up just for the drawing will open that one.

At the booth, the sign reads: **"Free evidence bag when you join the list. Placemat drawing at 4pm."**

**Collecting addresses:** use a clipboard sheet (`site/print/signup-sheet.html`, print it from a browser) plus a QR code to the sign-up form once the site is live. Type the paper list into your email tool that night, while you can still read the handwriting.

**Email tool:** MailerLite or Kit. Both have free plans that cover a list this size, and both send an automatic welcome email. Paste the tool's form URL into `SIGNUP_URL` in `site/index.html` and the website form will work.

## 2. Cadence: two a month, plus the holidays

Steady rhythm: **1st and 3rd Thursday of the month**, around 10am. Through the holidays add a few short bonus emails, then drop back to two a month in January.

| Date | Email | What's in it |
|---|---|---|
| **Sun Oct 18** (automatic) | Welcome | Free Mini Ridgeline pattern, what the name means, link to the runners on Etsy |
| **Mon Oct 19** | "We traced the call" | Placemat drawing winner, festival photos, thanks for stopping by |
| **Thu Oct 29** | "Have you checked the quilts?" | Halloween email: the horror movie behind the name, the last trick-or-treat bags |
| **Thu Nov 5** | Dye day | Process reel, new runner colorways. Subscribers get them 24 hours before Etsy |
| **Thu Nov 19** | Set the table | Thanksgiving table: runners and placemats styled, gift sets |
| **Sat Nov 28** | Small Business Saturday (short) | One photo, one link, one line |
| **Thu Dec 3** | Gift guide | Kits for the sewist, throws for everyone else. December placemat drawing |
| **Thu Dec 10** | Last call | Etsy shipping cutoff for Christmas (check Etsy's posted date) |
| **Thu Jan 7** | Minutes of the meeting | Year in review, what's next, QuiltCon plans |
| **Thu Jan 21** | From the vault | The 2012 unicycle post, reintroduced (see section 3) |

Every regular email has the same three parts, so it's quick to write:

1. **One story:** a process moment, a piece, a political quilt you admired (credit the maker).
2. **One thing to buy:** usually a runner or placemat set, linked to Etsy.
3. **One place to find you:** next fair, or "the shop is open 24/7."

Give the list something the public doesn't get: first look at new runners, the monthly placemat drawing, and the occasional pattern.

**Subject-line voice:** short, a little spooky, never clickbait. Examples:
- "We traced the call. It's coming from the dye bucket."
- "Have you checked the quilts?"
- "Don't go in the sewing room (new runners inside)"
- "Minutes of the last meeting"

## 3. The 2012 unicycle posts: on brand or not?

**On brand, with framing, in small doses.** The brand is personal, funny, and a little strange, and it already uses a "found footage" idea. A 2012 post about learning to ride a unicycle fits as a "From the vault" piece: it shows who you were before the quilts and how you learn things, by falling off until you don't, which is also how free-motion quilting goes.

How to use them:

- Run **one** of them as a "From the vault · 2012" journal post, with a new 2–3 sentence intro linking it to making. The site already has a slot for it.
- Use them in **slow months** (January), not the holiday selling season.
- Don't make "unicycles" a site category or an Instagram series. One or two posts is charming; ten would make it a different brand.
- Skip any post that's purely technical (gear, mileage) or that you'd now edit heavily.

*I couldn't find these posts in your Google Drive or Gmail. Send me the links or paste the text and I'll write the intros.*

## 4. Making YouTube and Instagram match

**YouTube (Kristi Sews Cool Stuff, 2021 sew-alongs):** frame these as "found footage" or "tapes from the basement." They're garment sewing, not art quilts, so:
- Cut 30–60 second vertical clips of the best moments (the "marshmallow in a microwave" pattern unfolding, French seams) and post them as Reels with a "VHS · 2021" label.
- Link the channel from the site footer, but don't lead with it.
- When you start posting quilt process videos, rename the channel to match the brand so everything lives under one name.

**Instagram (@artiscomingfrominsidethehouse):** keep three recurring series, and use the same names in the newsletter and on the site:
- **Dye Day:** cloth going in white and coming out blue. Your best-performing type of reel.
- **Ridge by Ridge:** a landscape built layer by layer, sped up.
- **Minutes of the Meeting:** protest and political quilts you admire, credited and tagged. This is where the QuiltCon spirit lives.

Use the logo palette in graphics and captions (sienna, luminous blue, grey blue, meadow green, olive, cherry charcoal, limewash cream). Put the typewriter-style text only on labels and captions, as the site does.

## 5. Runners and placemats on Etsy

Leading the Etsy shop with runners and placemats makes sense. They're quick to make, easy to photograph, and easy to gift. Watch for this at the festival:

- Which **colorways** people pick up first
- **Set size:** do people ask for 4 or 6 placemats?
- **Price reaction:** the costing sheet has no price for runners or placemats yet. Common handmade ranges are roughly $15–25 per placemat and $40–75 per runner, depending on size and whether it's hand dyed. Test one price at the booth and note what people say.
- **Length:** what size table people say they have

Add the winning colorways to Etsy first and announce them to the list 24 hours early (the Nov 5 email).

## 6. A note on the tone

The site leans into the sewing circle as a meeting with the door closed: "mild-mannered sewing circle, please ignore the agenda," with a nod to the protest quilts at QuiltCon. It keeps that to a wink and makes no historical claims. If you want to add the comparison to yoga spaces under British rule in India, do it as a personal-voice journal post that cites the source you read, so nobody can call it a stretch.
